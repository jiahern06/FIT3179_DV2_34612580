MALAYSIA COMMUNITY WELLBEING

Bridging the Divide

Exploring safety, digital inclusion and healthcare infrastructure across Malaysia.

This dashboard brings together public data to reveal how wellbeing differs across states and districts, and where communities may benefit most from better access to essential services.

Who Malaysians and community decision-makers What Safety, access and healthcare indicators Why To make regional inequalities easier to see

01 / PUBLIC SAFETY

Community peace and safety

Crime patterns vary considerably across Malaysia. These views show where incidents are concentrated and how state-level safety has changed over time.

District crime rates

Crime incidents per 10,000 residents in 2023.

Notice: Darker districts indicate higher crime rates relative to their population.

Finding: Crime is not evenly distributed: the map shows a clear contrast between higher-rate urban centres and lower-rate districts.

State crime trends

Crime rates across states from 2018 to 2023.

Notice: Compare colour intensity across years to see which states remain consistently higher or lower.

Finding: Kuala Lumpur starts as the strongest high-rate outlier, but its rate falls substantially by 2023; the national pattern is therefore changing rather than static.

Crime composition profiling

The offence categories contributing to Malaysia's overall crime profile.

Notice: The larger radial segments show which offence groups contribute most to the national total.

Finding: Property offences dominate the profile, with other theft, break-in and motorcycle theft forming the largest visible contributors.

Read the story: Start with the map to locate local differences, then use the heatmap to see whether those differences persist over time. The radial view explains which offence groups are driving the national picture.

02 / BASIC AMENITIES

Digital inclusion and essential amenities

Internet access and basic amenities are closely connected to opportunity. Compare regional gaps and follow how states have moved through the rankings.

Internet access and amenities

A bivariate view of digital access and amenities deficits.

Notice: Use the colour categories to find states facing both lower internet access and weaker basic amenities.

Finding: The combined map shows that digital access and basic amenities do not move together everywhere, revealing places where one need may be hidden by a stronger score on the other.

Progress path

The relationship between violent and property crime over time.

Follow the path: Each labelled point is a year, showing how the national crime mix changes over time.

Finding: Property crime remains much larger than violent crime throughout the period, while both measures shift noticeably across the years.

Urban–rural digital gap

The difference in household internet access by state in 2025.

Compare the dots: Longer connectors indicate a larger difference between urban and rural access.

Finding: The largest gaps occur in states where urban access is near the national ceiling but rural access remains visibly lower, making rural connectivity the main pressure point.

State ranking shifts

Changes in household internet access rankings from 2019 to 2025.

Follow the lines: Moving upward means a state has improved its relative position in the ranking.

Finding: Rankings are not fixed: several states exchange positions over time, while other states remain near the top or bottom of the comparison.

Read the story: The bivariate map identifies places facing two access challenges at once. The ranking and gap views then show how digital inclusion differs between states and between urban and rural households.

03 / HEALTHCARE INFRASTRUCTURE

Healthcare capacity and holistic wellbeing

Healthcare resources are not distributed evenly. These charts connect hospital capacity with a broader district-level view of community wellbeing.

Hospital beds by state

Hospital bed capacity and beds per 1,000 residents in 2022.

Read both channels: Circle size shows total capacity, while colour shows beds available per 1,000 residents.

Finding: Total capacity follows population size, but the colour scale shows that the largest hospital systems are not automatically the best supplied per resident.

Facility shifts over time

How hospital bed capacity has changed by facility type.

Read the bands: Changes in band thickness show how each facility type's share of capacity shifts over time.

Finding: MOH hospitals remain the largest share of beds and account for 79.2% in 2022, while non-MOH and special institutions make up the remainder.

Read the story: Hospital capacity is shown spatially first, then as a change in facility mix over time. This helps distinguish where capacity is located from how the national system is changing.

04 / HOLISTIC SYNTHESIS

How do districts compare overall?

The final view brings the indicators together so readers can compare districts without needing specialist statistical knowledge.

District profiles

Compare crime, internet, amenities and health indicators across 110 districts.

Explore a profile: Brush any axis to highlight districts with similar combinations of safety, access and healthcare indicators.

Finding: District wellbeing is multi-dimensional: a district can perform strongly on internet access while still differing on crime, amenities or hospital-bed provision.

What to take away: Brush across any axis to highlight districts with similar profiles. Use the tooltip to identify a district, then return to the earlier maps to understand its geographic context.

Domain and purpose

Community wellbeing in Malaysia. The visualisation helps a general audience identify geographic inequalities in safety, digital access and healthcare.

Sources and date

OpenDOSM, Ministry of Health Malaysia and Royal Malaysia Police. Data shown covers the latest available years in the supplied datasets; accessed October 2026.
